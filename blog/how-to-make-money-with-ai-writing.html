An editorial workflow for AI-assisted writing

AI can help a writer organize notes, generate outline options and improve a rough draft. It cannot take responsibility for the accuracy, originality or usefulness of the finished article. The strongest workflow keeps research and editorial decisions with a human author.

1. Define the assignment

Record the reader, purpose, format, length, tone and any required sources. For client work, agree on scope, deadlines, revision rounds, confidentiality and ownership before beginning.

2. Build a source pack

Collect reliable primary sources, product documentation, interviews or material supplied with permission. Note the date and relevant passage for each factual claim. Do not ask an AI system to invent sources or fill gaps with plausible-looking citations.

3. Ask for an outline, not a finished authority

Use a prompt such as: “Create a reader-focused outline from the notes below. Do not introduce facts not present in the notes. List unanswered questions and mark claims that need independent sources.” Compare the outline with the assignment and remove sections that do not help the reader.

4. Draft in manageable sections

Provide the assistant with the relevant source excerpts and the purpose of each section. Ask it to preserve uncertainty and distinguish quoted source material from interpretation. Rewrite generic passages and add original explanation, examples or analysis based on your own knowledge.

5. Run a substantive edit

  • Verify dates, names, numbers, quotations and technical claims.
  • Open cited sources and confirm that they support the statement.
  • Remove repetition, filler and unsupported superlatives.
  • Check that the headline accurately describes the content.
  • Review spelling, structure, accessibility and mobile readability.
  • Confirm that images and other assets are properly licensed.

6. Example: turn a weak paragraph into useful guidance

Weak draft: “AI makes writing faster and anyone can earn money from it.” This is broad, unsupported and offers no practical help.

More useful treatment: “A text assistant can suggest an outline or identify repeated phrases. The writer still needs to verify factual claims, check source permissions and edit for the intended audience. Test the workflow on one short piece and record the time spent on both drafting and review.”

The revised example is more specific, describes limitations and gives the reader an action to try.

Worked example: From a product brief to reviewed store copy

Example status: This is a practical demonstration completed for this guide using a fictional product brief. It is not a real client assignment, product listing or sales result. The purpose is to show how an editor can use an AI assistant while keeping factual decisions under human control.

Step 1: Record the source material

The brief describes a reusable desk bottle with a stainless-steel body, 500 ml capacity and screw-on lid. Available colors are black, white and blue. Hand washing is recommended. The package contains one bottle and one lid. Price, warranty, insulation, dimensions, weight and leak resistance are not specified.

Before drafting, the editor turns this into a short fact sheet. This prevents missing details from being silently filled with assumptions.

Step 2: Request an outline

Prompt used: “Create a concise outline for a product description based only on the source brief. Do not add performance claims or features. List missing details separately.”

Reviewed outline: product identity and material; capacity and lid type; available colors and package contents; care instructions; a short note listing specifications that the store owner has not supplied.

The editor keeps the outline because each section maps to information in the brief. No separate section is added for insulation or leak protection because neither feature is confirmed.

Step 3: Generate a first draft

AI-assisted first draft:

“Make your daily routine easier with this stylish, leakproof stainless-steel bottle. Its insulated design keeps drinks at the ideal temperature, while the secure screw-on lid makes it perfect for commuting. Choose from black, white or blue. The 500 ml bottle is easy to care for and comes with a lid.”

This draft reads smoothly, but it contains claims that the source does not support: “leakproof”, “insulated”, temperature performance, “secure” and suitability for commuting. It also uses promotional language without evidence. Fluency is not proof of accuracy.

Step 5: Produce the edited version

Revised product description:

“This reusable desk bottle is made from stainless steel and has a 500 ml capacity. It comes with a screw-on lid and is available in black, white and blue. Each package includes one bottle and one lid. Hand washing is recommended. Insulation performance, leak resistance, dimensions, weight, price and warranty details have not been provided; please confirm these specifications with the store before purchase.”

The revised copy is less promotional, but every included product detail can be traced to the brief. Missing specifications are disclosed instead of guessed.

Step 6: Keep an editorial change log

  • Removed “leakproof” and “secure” because no leak test or closure-performance information was supplied.
  • Removed insulation and temperature claims because insulation was not specified.
  • Removed the commuting claim because intended use was not established by the brief.
  • Retained the material, capacity, lid type, colors, package contents and hand-wash recommendation.
  • Added a clear note that important commercial and physical specifications remain unknown.

What this example demonstrates

The assistant helped create an outline and a readable first draft, but the editor had to compare each claim with the source, remove unsupported statements and make missing information visible. This example does not measure time saved or prove that AI is faster than unaided writing; no timed comparison was conducted.

7. Protect client information

Do not paste confidential drafts, customer details or unpublished business information into an external service unless the client has authorized it and you have reviewed the provider's data terms. Keep a record of source material and permissions. Follow any client, employer or platform disclosure requirements for AI assistance.

8. Evaluate the workflow

Compare the final quality, total production time and correction effort with your previous process. A faster first draft is not a benefit if fact-checking and revisions take longer. For service work, track communication and revision time as well as writing time.
