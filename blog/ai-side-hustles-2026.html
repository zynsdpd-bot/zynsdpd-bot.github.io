AI-assisted side projects: four ideas and how to validate them

AI tools can lower the effort involved in research, drafting, editing and repetitive production. They do not create customer demand by themselves. A useful side project starts with a problem someone already has and a deliverable you can quality-check.

This guide compares four service ideas and gives you a small validation exercise. It does not estimate earnings: prices, demand and legal requirements vary by market, experience and scope.

1. Editing and content preparation

Possible deliverable: turn a client's approved notes into a structured FAQ, newsletter draft or product description, then edit it for clarity and consistency.

Human skills required: subject familiarity, writing, source checking and the ability to recognize unsupported statements.

Small test: create a sample using your own material. Ask three people in the intended audience whether the result answers a real question and what they would change. Track research and revision time, not just drafting time.

2. Video post-production support

Possible deliverable: a clean short clip with corrected captions, sensible pacing and platform-specific exports from footage supplied by the client.

Human skills required: storytelling, audio judgment, caption correction and media-rights awareness.

Small test: edit a 30–60 second clip from footage you own or are licensed to use. Check every caption against the audio, watch it on a phone and note how long revisions take.

3. Design production assistance

Possible deliverable: a set of social post layouts or a simple presentation based on an approved brand brief.

Human skills required: hierarchy, typography, accessibility, brand consistency and file preparation.

Small test: create two alternative layouts for a fictional brief. Ask reviewers to identify the intended message and compare legibility at mobile size. Check the commercial-use terms for every generated or stock asset.

4. Small workflow documentation and automation

Possible deliverable: document a repeatable administrative process or prototype a low-risk workflow that prepares drafts for human approval.

Human skills required: process mapping, testing, permissions and troubleshooting.

Small test: map the current process first. Use sample data and a read-only prototype. Test missing fields, duplicate events and service outages before allowing any external action.

Worked example: A simulated product-description micro-project

Example status: This is a simulated project for learning purposes, not a real shop, paying client, customer interview or completed commercial job. The product and brief are fictional. No sales, customer demand, market price or income result is claimed.

1. Define the small deliverable

Scenario: A small online shop needs one concise product description and three short FAQ answers for a reusable desk bottle. The proposed deliverable is limited to one product page draft and one fact-check sheet. It does not include photography, advertising, SEO ranking promises or customer support.

2. Record the supplied product facts

  • Material: stainless steel; capacity: 500 ml.
  • Lid: screw-on.
  • Colors: black, white and blue.
  • Care: hand wash recommended.
  • Package: one bottle and one lid.
  • Price, warranty, insulation, dimensions, weight and leak resistance: not specified.

3. Generate a first draft

Illustrative AI draft:

“Stay refreshed wherever you go with our premium insulated stainless-steel bottle. The leakproof screw-top design keeps drinks hot or cold for hours. Choose from black, white or blue and enjoy a durable 500 ml bottle for everyday adventures.”

This draft is fluent but unsuitable for publication. “Premium”, “insulated”, “leakproof”, “keeps drinks hot or cold for hours” and “durable” are not supported by the fictional brief. “Wherever you go” and “everyday adventures” also imply a use case that has not been established.

4. Apply human edits

5. Prepare the edited deliverable

Revised product description:

“This reusable desk bottle is made from stainless steel and has a 500 ml capacity. It comes with a screw-on lid and is available in black, white and blue. Each package includes one bottle and one lid. Hand washing is recommended. Please contact the store to confirm the price, warranty, dimensions and any insulation or leak-resistance specifications.”

Sample FAQ:

  • What is the bottle made from? It is made from stainless steel.
  • How should I clean it? Hand washing is recommended.
  • Does it keep drinks hot or cold? Insulation performance is not specified in the supplied product information. Please confirm with the store.

6. Define review and handover

The fact sheet, edited description and FAQ draft are the proposed handover. Before publication, the shop owner would need to confirm missing specifications, approve the wording and provide any product images with appropriate usage rights. A real project would also require an agreed scope, deadline, revision limit and permission to use the supplied materials.

7. Track effort without inventing results

A project log should record research time, prompting and drafting time, fact-checking, revisions, client communication, software charges and any licensed asset costs. No time or cost figures are reported here because this is a simulated exercise, not a timed client pilot.

What the simulation teaches: A narrow product-copy task can be described as a concrete deliverable, but AI-generated marketing language needs close review. The useful service is not simply generating text; it is producing a checked draft, identifying missing information and handing over clear questions for the product owner.

A seven-step validation checklist

  1. Name one specific audience and problem.
  2. Talk to potential users before building a service.
  3. Write a one-sentence scope and list what is excluded.
  4. Produce one sample using material you have permission to use.
  5. Ask for feedback on usefulness, accuracy and turnaround.
  6. Calculate software costs, research time, revisions and administration.
  7. Run a small paid pilot only when scope, rights and delivery expectations are clear.

Do not promise a particular income, client volume or platform result. Check local tax and consumer rules and the current terms of any marketplace you use.
