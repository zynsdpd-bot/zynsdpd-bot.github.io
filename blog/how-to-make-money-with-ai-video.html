Producing and reviewing an AI-assisted video

AI video features can help with transcription, rough edits, captions, background cleanup and early visual concepts. A publishable video still needs a clear purpose, accurate information, coherent pacing and permission to use every asset.

1. Start with a one-page brief

  • Audience: who should understand this?
  • Purpose: what is the one key takeaway?
  • Format: vertical short, horizontal explainer or another format?
  • Length: what is the shortest duration that communicates the idea?
  • Call to action: what should the viewer do next, if anything?

2. Write a short script

Organize the script into a hook, a few explanatory points and a conclusion. Ask an AI assistant for alternative structures, not unsupported facts. Mark every factual claim that needs a source and verify it before recording or generating visuals.

Example prompt: “Turn these verified notes into a 45-second explainer script. Do not add facts beyond the notes. Mark any unclear statement with [CHECK]. Use plain language and include a visual suggestion for each sentence.”

3. Build a shot list

For each sentence, decide whether you need original footage, a screen recording, a diagram, a licensed stock clip or a generated visual. Avoid generic filler footage that does not help explain the point. If a synthetic scene could be mistaken for documentary evidence, label or contextualize it appropriately.

4. Edit and correct captions

  1. Make a rough cut that removes pauses and repeated material.
  2. Generate captions, then compare every line with the actual audio.
  3. Correct names, numbers, technical terms and punctuation.
  4. Check caption timing, contrast and safe areas on a phone screen.
  5. Listen with headphones for clipping, noise and abrupt transitions.
  6. Watch the final export once without pausing.

Worked example: Planning a 45-second fact-checking explainer

Example status: This is a production-planning and editorial-review demonstration, not a claim that a finished video was rendered or published. The script and shot list below were prepared for this guide. No audience results, production-time measurements or platform performance are claimed.

Step 1: Write the brief

  • Audience: Beginners who use AI assistants to draft articles or product descriptions.
  • Purpose: Show one simple habit: verify factual claims against the original material before publishing.
  • Format: Vertical 9:16 short-form explainer.
  • Target length: Approximately 45 seconds.
  • Takeaway: Treat AI output as a draft, not as a source of evidence.

Step 2: Draft and review the voice-over

Drafting prompt: “Write a voice-over for a 45-second beginner-friendly video explaining how to check factual claims in AI-generated text. Use only these points: compare claims with the original source, verify names and numbers, and remove or qualify unsupported statements. Do not promise that checking guarantees accuracy. Include a visual idea for each beat.”

Reviewed voice-over script:

  1. 0–6 seconds — Hook: “AI can produce a confident-sounding answer. But confidence is not evidence.”
  2. 6–16 seconds — Check the source: “Take each important claim and compare it with the original document, product brief or reliable source.”
  3. 16–27 seconds — Check details: “Verify names, dates and numbers. Open cited links yourself; a citation in an AI answer is not automatically proof.”
  4. 27–38 seconds — Handle gaps: “If the source does not confirm a claim, remove it or say clearly that the detail is unknown.”
  5. 38–45 seconds — Close: “Use AI for a first draft. Keep fact-checking and the final decision in human hands.”

The script is designed for an estimated 45-second delivery. The timings are an editorial target, not a measured recording result; the final duration would need to be checked against the recorded voice-over.

Step 4: Prepare and proofread captions

Caption text:

AI can produce a confident-sounding answer.
But confidence is not evidence.
Compare each important claim with the original source.
Verify names, dates and numbers.
Open cited links yourself.
If a detail is not confirmed, remove it or mark it as unknown.
Use AI for a first draft.
Verify before publishing.

These are condensed caption lines for readability, not a timed subtitle file. Before export, synchronize each line to the actual audio, check spelling against the script, and review line breaks and contrast on a phone screen.

Step 5: Run the editorial and rights checklist

  • Check that the voice-over does not claim that fact-checking guarantees a perfect result.
  • Confirm that every example on screen is either original material or clearly labelled as an illustration.
  • Use self-created text cards and screen recordings, or assets with documented permissions.
  • Do not add background music, stock footage, fonts or voice clones without checking their licence and applicable consent requirements.
  • Watch the complete export on a phone and compare every caption with the audio.
  • Confirm the final runtime, audio levels, visual safe areas and platform disclosure requirements before publishing.

Step 6: Record what has and has not been tested

The brief, script, shot list, caption copy and editorial checklist form a reusable production plan. A rendered video, synchronized subtitle file, rights-cleared media package and audience performance data would still need to be produced and checked in an actual editing workflow. This demonstration therefore does not report a finished export, measured editing time, viewer retention or monetization outcome.

Lesson: AI can help structure a short video, but a usable production still depends on source verification, accurate captions, appropriate media rights and a final human review.

5. Check rights and consent

Confirm permission for footage, photographs, music, fonts, voice recordings and likenesses. Read the current tool terms for commercial use, attribution and restrictions. Obtain appropriate consent before cloning or simulating a real person's voice or appearance. Follow the destination platform's current rules for altered or synthetic media.

6. Understand monetization limits

Publishing a video does not automatically make it eligible for advertising revenue. Platforms may apply eligibility thresholds, originality requirements, advertiser-friendly rules and disclosure policies. Repetitive, minimally transformed or mass-produced material may not qualify. Check the platform's current official policies rather than relying on old tutorials.

7. Evaluate the pilot

For an initial test, measure production time, correction effort, viewer retention and useful audience feedback. Compare the result with a video made using your previous workflow. Do not infer future earnings from one upload or a short period of views.
