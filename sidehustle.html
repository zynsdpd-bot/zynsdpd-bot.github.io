Practical ways to apply AI skills to small projects

AI can reduce time spent on parts of a workflow, but customers pay for useful outcomes—not for the fact that a tool was used. The examples below are starting points to validate, not promises of earnings.

1. Editing and content support

Possible deliverables include proofreading, restructuring a client-provided draft, creating a content brief or preparing social captions. You still need strong language skills, fact-checking and a clear understanding of the client's audience.

Small validation test

  1. Choose one industry you understand.
  2. Create a before-and-after sample using material you own or have permission to use.
  3. Ask a few potential customers whether the deliverable solves a real problem.
  4. Estimate research, editing and revision time before setting a price.

2. Short-form video support

AI-assisted transcription, captioning and rough cuts can speed up editing. A useful service may include a clean edit, accurate subtitles and platform-ready exports. Confirm that clients own or have licensed the footage, music and voice recordings.

3. Design production support

AI can help generate mood boards or early concepts. Final work still requires layout judgment, typography, brand consistency and checks on the tool's commercial-use terms. Avoid presenting generated concepts as exclusive assets unless the rights and originality are clear.

4. Small-business workflow setup

Map a repetitive process such as turning an approved form submission into a draft task. Start with a low-risk workflow, document failure cases and require human approval before sending messages, changing records or making payments.

How to validate an idea responsibly

  • Interview potential users before building a product.
  • Offer a clearly scoped pilot and collect feedback.
  • Track all costs and unpaid time.
  • Check tax, consumer, privacy and marketplace obligations in your jurisdiction.
  • Do not guarantee earnings, rankings or business outcomes.
